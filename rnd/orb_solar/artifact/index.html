What each direction does

  

    

      
A — Solar Intelligence

      
A photosphere built from domain-warped mottling with ridged fibril crests, a limb that darkens the way a real stellar disc does, and a corona clumped into a handful of curved streamers rather than an even halo.

      
Strong: the most composed and the cheapest. Weak: the silhouette stays a circle, so at low energy it is the closest of the four to an ordinary glowing ball.


      
B — Ember Storm

      
Not a disc with decoration on top. The flame is a single field whose outer boundary varies with angle and time, so the outline is genuinely ragged, and three parallax layers of embers drift across the whole frame.

      
Strong: by far the most convincing as fire, and the only one that never reads as a circle. Weak: turbulence without intent — it looks energetic rather than aware — and it is the most expensive.

    

    

      
C — Magnetic Sun

      
The photosphere from A, plus four magnetic loop arcs anchored on the limb. Each arc is explicit geometry on its own rise-hold-decay lifecycle, with plasma knots travelling between its footpoints.

      
Strong: the arcs are the only motion in the set that reads as deliberate, which is what makes the orb feel like a presence. Weak: the limb itself stays circular.


      
D — Refined

      
The improvement pass on the strongest direction: C's arcs and photosphere, with B's silhouette breach gated behind energy. At idle the limb is clean and composed; as energy rises it erupts.

      
Strong: it has somewhere to go. The orb is calm when calm and violent when busy, so the state is legible without reading any label. Weak: at peak energy the core still flattens toward white, and the corona can drift smoky.

This is a high-end discrete GPU. Integrated and mobile parts run heavy fragment work roughly 10–30× slower, so D at 900² could cost 6–17 ms there. Cap the backing resolution rather than the logical size, and drop the ember layer count on weak hardware.


  
Recommendation

  

    
Ship D as a Flutter FragmentShader. No Blender, no particle system, no video. One fragment program, one drawRect, ten float uniforms; the ember field is analytic in the shader, so there is nothing to simulate on the CPU and no overdraw.

    
The shader in this page and the .frag in the Flutter app are generated from one source, so the prototype cannot drift from what ships.

  


  
How the states work

  
All five states are points in the same six-parameter space — energy, turbulence, flare, ember density, focus and exposure — so the orb can move between any two without a cut. Nothing is a separate animation.

  
    
    
  • idle — slow breathing on three incommensurate periods, clean limb, sparse embers.
    • 
    
  • listening — brighter and more coherent; focus rises, so the arcs tighten.
    • 
    
  • thinking — turbulence up and focus down: the surface churns and the arcs thicken and loosen.
    • 
    
  • speaking — flare activity dominates; prominences rise and fall while the surface stays composed.
    • 
    
  • acting — everything open. The limb breaches, embers fill the frame, arcs reach furthest.
