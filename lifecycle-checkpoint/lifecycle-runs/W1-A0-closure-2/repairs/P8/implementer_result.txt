I fixed R14-01 by addressing the root cause, not just the reviewer's strings. I did not include all 214 Kangxi radicals, as the separate diagnosis proposed; the reason is below.

**Root cause (confirmed in the source).** `normalise()` converts all text to a canonical form (NFKC) before any check, at `uri_web/untrusted.py:255`. That turns the Kangxi radical forms into ordinary characters (`⼁`→`丨`, `⼀`→`一`). But the allow-list of stroke and dot letters, `_STROKES`, was written using the Kangxi forms, which never appear in checked text. The ordinary forms of the one-stroke radicals (`一 丨 乙`) were also missing. As a result, `丨丶`, `一丶`, `乙丶` and similar were read as the name. Most of the old list could never match. The CJK Strokes range U+31C0–31E3 was dead too, because those characters are symbols, not letters, and so never form part of a word.

**The fix (`uri_web/untrusted.py`, `_STROKES`).**
- The set is now built by passing each character through `normalise()`, so it is in the same form as the checked text.
- It keeps only results that are a single letter, and a module-level assertion checks that every member is unchanged by `normalise()` and is a letter.
- Members: the one-stroke radicals (`一丨丶丿乙亅`, including their Kangxi forms U+2F00–2F05), the stroke variants `乀乁乚乛`, the stroke-and-dot radicals `二 亠`, the Hangul araea `ᆞ ᆢ` (with `ㆍ`, which normalises to `ᆞ`), and `〆`.
- The dead CJK Strokes range and the non-letters (`・`, `〇`) are removed. They already count as plain gap between words, so nothing is lost.

**Why not all 214 radicals.** Their normalised forms include real words such as `人`, `口`, `山`. Adding them would make ordinary text like "Yamaguchi 山口 is in Japan" read as a link. That breaks D17's rule that real short foreign words are names, and the case is now a control in the new test. The leftover risk: a pair of radicals that are real words, written in Kangxi form, is identical to ordinary text once normalised. It therefore falls under the residual the owner already accepted ("a real short foreign word is the name"). Nothing new is opened.

**Regression test: `tests/web/test_w1_closure_r14_review_regressions.py`** (self-contained, no fixtures).
- It checks the rule itself, not only the reviewer's strings:
  - The allow-list is in canonical form and covers the normalised Kangxi one-stroke radicals.
  - Every two-letter combination of the stroke set, ordinary and Kangxi forms included (400 pairs), is caught by `has_link_or_contact` and removed by `mask_links`.
- The reviewer's forms plus `二丶` and `亠丶` are also checked through `verify`, `extractive`, `build_surface` and `require_valid`.
- Controls that must stay readable: `東京 is`, `(東京)`, `山口 is`, `서울 is`. Two owner-accepted residuals are confirmed as unchanged: `中国`, and a stroke pair in brackets (`(丨丶)`).
