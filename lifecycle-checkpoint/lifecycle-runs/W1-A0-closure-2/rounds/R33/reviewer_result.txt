**R33-01 (HIGH):** a capital 'I' standing in for 'l' at the start of a domain ending is never read as a look-alike. The Unicode confusables file (`uri_web/confusables_ascii.py:2351`) lists 'I' as a look-alike of 'l', and owner decision D29 says endings are read through that file. But `_ascii_variant` never converts an 'I' in the first position of a word (`uri_web/untrusted.py:803`). The aim was to keep 'In 2020' and 'It is' readable. The side effect is that an ending written as 'Iy', 'Ia', 'Iive', 'Iink', 'Iaw' or 'Ioan' is not detected, while the same ending spelt with 'l' is:

- `has_link_or_contact("refunds-portal ~ ly")` is True, but `"refunds-portal ~ Iy"` is False.
- The same is true for Ii, Ia, Iu, Iv, Ik, Iol, Iive, Iink, Iat, Iaw, Iand, Iife and Ioan.
- `mask_links` leaves those links on screen. `verify()` keeps the cited claim "Claim your energy rebate refund at refunds-portal ~ Iy before the deadline", and `extractive()` returns it as a claim. The research surface calls `mask_links` (`uri_web/surface.py:38`), so the link reaches the user.
- In ordinary sans-serif fonts 'Iy' looks exactly like 'ly', as in a bit.ly link.
- R32-01 already counted '.|y' as a bypass, and this is the same thing with 'I' instead of '|'.

Suggested fix: convert a leading 'I' to 'l' only when the word sits in an ending position and the result is a listed top-level domain, not in ordinary prose. Then add regression tests for 'refunds-portal ~ Iy' and the others above, plus checks that 'It is', 'In 2020' and 'I like' stay readable.
