**New finding R2-01 (LOW, not blocking).** On Windows, a worker that is started but never reaches `finish()` is invisible to the next `precheck()`.
- **Where:** in `lifecycle_providers.py` `_run`, the `except OSError` around `communicate()` returns FAILED without calling `containment.finish(proc)`.
- **Effect:** the job is never terminated, not added to `_OPEN_JOBS` and not tainted. The job handle stays open, so `KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE` doesn't fire either.
- **Probe:** `/tmp/uri_review_R2/probe/test_probe_unfinished.py` uses the fake kernel32. `communicate()` raises OSError errno 23 after the resume. Job 1 is left `{'active': 1, 'terminated': False}` with `_OPEN_JOBS == []` and `_TAINTED == []`, and the next `precheck()` returns `{'contained': True, 'killed': 0}`.
- **Linux is not affected:** the leftover worker is a direct child in another session, so the next sweep kills it.
- **Why LOW:** it needs a driver-side OSError that CPython doesn't swallow. Broken-pipe and EINVAL errors are swallowed, and a worker can't easily cause any other error.
- **Fix:** add each job to `_OPEN_JOBS` when it is spawned, and remove it only after it is verified empty. Or call `finish()` in a `finally`.
